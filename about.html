About Us

Phone Screen Resolution is a free web tool with one job: tell you exactly what screen you are looking at — its resolution, its size in CSS pixels, its pixel density and its orientation.

The need is real. Every phone advertises a resolution, but finding the number on your own device means digging through Settings menus that change name with every brand and every update. And the advertised number is only half the story: the viewport size decides how websites look, the pixel ratio decides how sharp text feels, and PPI decides whether you can see individual pixels at all. This page puts all of those numbers in front of you the moment it loads.

That is why we built this tool for the browser. Open the page and the numbers are already there — no app, no download, no account. It works on phones, tablets, laptops and desktops, because every one of them has a browser.

What the tool does

  • Live resolution readout — your physical screen resolution in pixels, read straight from the device.
  • Viewport size — the size in CSS pixels that websites actually use to lay out pages.
  • Pixel ratio (DPR) — how many physical pixels make up each CSS pixel.
  • Color depth and orientation — bits per pixel and portrait or landscape, updated live as you rotate.
  • PPI calculator — enter your screen's diagonal size in inches to get its pixels per inch.
  • Copy details — copy the full readout in one tap to share it or paste it into a listing.

Who we are

We also run the SpeakerCleaners tool and the White Screen test. Phone Screen Resolution is our third free tool — same idea, different question: instead of cleaning a speaker or testing a display, it simply tells you what your screen is.

Our promise

The tool is free and will stay free. There are no accounts, no subscriptions, no locked features and no downloads. We do not collect your data — the numbers are read on your own device, in your browser, and nothing leaves it.

If the tool helped you check a new phone — or settle an argument about which phone is sharper — share it with someone shopping for their next screen.
