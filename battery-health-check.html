How to check real battery health

Your phone keeps the real health number in its settings. Here is where to find it:

📱 On Android

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Battery.
  3. Tap Battery health (on some phones: Battery usage → three dots → Battery health).
  4. Read the health percentage or capacity estimate.

🍎 On iPhone

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Battery.
  3. Tap Battery Health & Charging.
  4. Read Maximum Capacity — that is your battery health %.

⚠️ Swollen battery? Stop and get help

If your phone's back cover is bulging, the screen is lifting away from the frame, or the phone feels puffy, your battery may be swollen. Do not charge it, press on it or try to open the phone yourself. Take it to a repair shop as soon as possible — a swollen battery can overheat or catch fire.

What is battery health?

Battery health is how much charge your battery can still hold compared to when it was new. A fresh battery holds 100% of its design capacity; as it ages, that number slowly drops, and the phone dies sooner on every charge.

🔋 Battery capacity

Measured in milliamp-hours (mAh). A phone rated at 5,000 mAh holds that much when new — after two years of use it may only hold 4,000 mAh or less.

🔁 Charge cycles

One cycle means using 100% of the battery's charge, in one go or spread over days. Most phone batteries are built for around 500 full cycles before wear becomes noticeable.

📉 Battery wear

Every charge slowly wears the battery's chemistry down. Heat, fast charging and deep discharges speed that wear up — which is why old phones die so fast.

⚡ Why it matters

A worn battery means shorter days, sudden shutdowns and sometimes slower performance, because the phone throttles itself to protect a weak battery.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check my phone's battery health?

Open this page for a live reading of your battery level, charging status and time left. For the true health percentage, check your settings: on Android go to Settings → Battery → Battery health; on iPhone go to Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging and read Maximum Capacity.

What is a good battery health percentage?

Above 90% is excellent, 80–90% is good and normal after a year or two, and below 80% means the battery is worn — Apple itself considers below 80% the point to think about a replacement. Below 70% usually means very short battery life and sudden shutdowns.

Can a website really test my battery health?

Only partly, and honestly: a website can read your live battery level, whether you are charging, and the time to full or empty. It cannot read design capacity or charge cycles, so no website can give a true health percentage. That number only lives in your phone's own settings.

Why does my battery drain so fast?

Usually it is a mix of an ageing battery, a bright screen, poor signal, and apps running in the background. Check which apps use the most battery in Settings → Battery, and turn on battery saver if you need the day to last longer.

How many charge cycles does a phone battery last?

Most phone batteries are rated for about 500 full charge cycles before their capacity drops to roughly 80%. A cycle is 100% of charge used — so charging from 50% to 100% twice counts as one cycle, not two.

Is it bad to charge my phone overnight?

Modern phones stop charging at 100% and trickle instead, so overnight charging is not as harmful as it once was. Still, keeping a battery at 100% for hours every night adds a little wear. If your phone has an "optimised charging" setting, turn it on — it waits to finish charging until just before you wake up.

Should I let my battery drain to 0%?

No — deep discharges wear lithium batteries out faster. Try to keep your battery between about 20% and 80% most of the time. A full drain once in a while is fine, but do not make it a habit.

Does fast charging damage the battery?

A little, over time. Fast charging creates more heat, and heat is the battery's biggest enemy. It is fine to use when you need a quick top-up, but for everyday overnight charging a slower charger is gentler on the battery.

When should I replace my phone battery?

When the health percentage drops below 80%, when the phone shuts off early, or when you tick three or more signs in the self-check above. A battery replacement is usually much cheaper than a new phone and can make an old phone feel new again.

Is this battery health check free?

Yes, completely free. The live battery reading, the real-health guide and the self-check list are all free, with no limits and no account.

Tips to make your battery last longer

🔋 Stay between 20% and 80%

Lithium batteries wear slowest in the middle of their range. Avoid letting the phone sit at 0% or 100% for long stretches.

🌡️ Keep it cool

Heat ages batteries faster than anything else. Do not leave your phone in a hot car or in direct sun while charging.

🔌 Use the right charger

Cheap, uncertified chargers can overheat the battery. Use the charger that came with your phone, or a certified one from a known brand.

🔆 Turn the brightness down

The screen is the biggest battery drain on most phones. Lower brightness, or use dark mode on OLED screens, and the battery lasts noticeably longer.

📴 Tame background apps

Check Settings → Battery to see which apps use the most power, and restrict the ones you rarely use. Battery saver mode helps on long days.

🔄 Update your software

System updates often include battery optimisations and fix apps that drain power in the background. Keep your phone up to date.
